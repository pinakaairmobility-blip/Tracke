2.1 — UDCPR 2020 Overview

The Unified Development Control and Promotion Regulations (UDCPR) 2020, notified by the Maharashtra government on 5th December 2020, replaced all previous DCRs for municipal corporations (except Mumbai). It is the single binding regulation governing building design, FSI, setbacks, and use in Nashik. Your architect must work within UDCPR 2020 — not any older Nashik DCR.

Important: Verify that your plot falls within NMC limits and not in a Nashik Rural or Nashik Planning Authority zone. The governing regulation differs — if in doubt, check the Development Plan of Nashik (available at NMC office) and verify the zone: Residential Zone (R1 or R2), which determines permitted uses and FSI.

2.3 — TDR — What to Know Before Buying
TDR Guidance

How TDR Works Practically in Nashik

  • TDR certificates are issued by NMC/MIDC/government to landowners who surrender land for roads, amenities, reservations
  • These certificates are tradeable — you buy them from existing TDR holders through a market
  • In Nashik, TDR market is active — your architect or a local property consultant can identify sellers
  • TDR must be of the same zone or receiving zone as your plot — residential TDR for residential project
  • TDR is registered via an agreement of TDR utilization submitted with your building plan
  • The amount of TDR you can use: up to 50% of basic FSI (i.e., 0.50 additional FSI for a basic FSI of 1.50)
  • Always have your advocate verify TDR certificates before purchase — fake TDR is a known fraud

3.2 — Structural Engineer & Soil Investigation
Step 3.2a

Structural Engineer Appointment

The structural engineer is legally separate from the architect. Their role: design foundation, columns, beams, slabs, retaining walls, and provide stability certificate at each construction stage. Requirements:

  • Must hold a degree in Civil/Structural Engineering from a recognized university
  • Must be licensed/empaneled with NMC as a Licensed Technical Person (LTP) under MRTP Act
  • Signs and stamps all structural drawings — this creates personal legal liability
Step 3.2b

Soil Investigation (Geotechnical Survey)

The soil test determines the safe bearing capacity of the ground and the appropriate foundation system. For a G+4 building on a plot in Dasak Shiwar (which may have varied soil conditions near the road corridor), this is critical. The report must include:

  • Bore log data: depth of drilling, soil type at each layer (sandy, clayey, rocky, filled)
  • SPT values (N-values): Standard Penetration Test at every 1.5m depth — determines soil strength
  • Safe Bearing Capacity (SBC): in kN/sqm — the structural engineer uses this to design footings
  • Water table depth: determines whether you need waterproofing in basement/plinth
  • Foundation recommendation: isolated footings, combined footings, raft, or pile (piles needed if SBC is low)

Minimum 3–4 bore holes for a plot of this size, spread across the footprint. Report must be signed by a qualified geotechnical engineer.

NOC 4.1 — Fire Department NOC (Mandatory — G+4)
NOC 4.1 — Fire Department, NMC

Fire NOC — Documents, System Specs, Inspection Process

Documents to submit:

  • Application letter addressed to Chief Fire Officer, NMC
  • Fire protection plan (from your AutoDCR drawing set)
  • Structural drawings showing staircase and escape route widths
  • Specifications of fire fighting equipment proposed

Fire system specifications required for G+4 residential (Maharashtra Fire Prevention & Life Safety Measures Act 2006):

  • Wet Riser System: 100mm (4") diameter GI pipe running vertically through all floors. Landing valve on each floor. Siamese connection on ground floor for fire brigade connection. Pressurized via fire pump.
  • Terrace / Overhead Water Tank: Min 10,000 litres dedicated for fire fighting (separate from domestic supply). Clearly marked.
  • Fire Pump: Electric pump (main) + diesel pump (standby) of min 900 LPM capacity at 3.5 kg/cm² pressure. Pump room at ground level with 24-hour access.
  • Hose Reel Cabinet: On each floor landing — 20m hose + nozzle. Cabinet in break-glass enclosure.
  • Portable Fire Extinguishers: ABC dry powder type, 5kg minimum. One per 150 sqm of floor area. Also CO2 extinguishers in electrical rooms.
  • Smoke Detectors: In lift lobby, common passages, and electrical rooms. Interconnected alarm system.
  • Emergency Exit Signage: Illuminated green "EXIT" signs above all escape routes. Backup battery operated.
  • Fire Escape Staircase: Min 1.0m wide, no combustible material stored, self-closing fire door at each floor landing, leads directly to open ground level.
  • Fire Doors: At lift lobby on each floor — min 60-minute fire rating (FD 60).

Inspection process: After drawings are approved, fire officer conducts a site inspection during construction to verify systems are being installed per approved drawings. Final inspection before OC.

📍 Chief Fire Officer, NMC, Nashik⏱ 30–60 days for drawing approval; final NOC at completion💰 Nominal govt fee + installation cost ₹3–8 Lakhs

Step 5.3 — NMC Technical Scrutiny

What Happens During NMC Review

After online submission and fee payment, NMC's Town Planning department assigns a technical officer who reviews the drawings manually for:

  • Conformity with the Nashik Development Plan zone classification
  • Correct application of UDCPR 2020 regulations relevant to your specific zone
  • Accuracy of FSI calculation including TDR and premium FSI claims
  • Validity of all NOCs submitted
  • Structural drawing adequacy

If the officer raises written objections (Sakshep Patrak), your architect must respond within the time stipulated (usually 30 days) with revised drawings or written justification. The file gets rejected if you miss the response deadline — you would need to resubmit from scratch.

Common Objections to Prepare For: FSI exceeds limit without adequate TDR proof; parking count short by 1–2 ECS; fire staircase width insufficient; rainwater harvesting plan missing; solar provision not shown; setback measurement error.
Step 5.4 — Building Plan Sanction Letter

What the Sanction Letter Contains

Once all objections are cleared and fees paid, NMC issues the formal Building Plan Sanction letter. It specifies:

  • Approved FSI and built-up area
  • Number of floors permitted
  • Conditions of sanction (e.g., fire NOC final at completion, OC before possession)
  • Validity period — usually 5 years from sanction date; must apply for CC within this period
  • Any deviations that were conditionally approved subject to payment

Get 5 certified copies of the sanction letter. You will need them for: MahaRERA, bank project approval, Commencement Certificate, MSEDCL connection, and buyer documentation.

Step 6.3 — Escrow Account (70% Rule)

Designated Project Bank Account — Mandatory

Under Section 4(2)(l)(D) of RERA 2016, 70% of all amounts realized from buyers must be deposited in a separate designated bank account and can only be withdrawn in proportion to the percentage of project completion certified by an engineer, architect, and CA.

  • Open a separate current account in a scheduled bank specifically for this project — name it "Indraprastha Heights Project Account"
  • This account must be disclosed on RERA portal
  • Every booking amount, installment, and payment from buyers: 70% must go into this account
  • The remaining 30% can be used for land cost, overheads, marketing
  • Withdrawals from the 70% account require a certificate from your structural engineer (% completion), architect, and CA
  • Bank cannot allow withdrawals without these certificates
Do not mix project funds with personal account or other projects. RERA auditors and MahaRERA inspections specifically check this. Violation leads to registration cancellation and refund liability to all buyers.
Step 6.4 — Carpet Area & Pricing Rules Under RERA

How to Calculate and Declare Carpet Area

RERA Section 2(k) defines carpet area as: "the net usable floor area of an apartment, excluding the area covered by the external walls, areas under services shafts, exclusive balcony or verandah area and exclusive open terrace area, but includes the area covered by the internal partition walls of the apartment."

What is EXCLUDED from Carpet Area
External walls + Shared walls + Service shafts + Balconies + Terraces = NOT carpet area
What is INCLUDED: All internal rooms + internal partition walls + internal bathroom/toilet + internal passages
You MUST price flats per sqft of carpet area — not super built-up area. Buyers have a legal right to demand carpet area pricing.

Typically, carpet area = 70–75% of super built-up area. For your plan, if a 2BHK has 850 sqft super built-up, carpet area would be approximately 595–638 sqft. Declare the carpet area accurately in RERA — giving a lower carpet area than actual is prohibited; giving higher is also prohibited.

Step 6.5 — Agreement for Sale (AFS) — Mandatory Clauses

What Every Agreement for Sale Must Include

Under RERA and Maharashtra MOFA 1963, every agreement for sale of a flat must contain these clauses in prescribed format (MahaRERA has a model AFS format):

  • Exact carpet area of the flat being sold
  • Price per sqft of carpet area + separately stated common area charges
  • Payment schedule linked to construction milestones (not arbitrary demands)
  • Proposed date of possession
  • Specification of materials — flooring, tiles, fittings, windows (model specifications)
  • Penalty clause: if builder delays — interest at SBI MCLR + 2% per month on amount paid
  • Buyer's right to cancel and get refund with interest if builder delays by more than agreed period
  • RERA registration number of the project
  • Clause confirming the promoter will not mortgage the flat after AFS is signed

The AFS must be registered at the Sub-Registrar office. Stamp duty on AFS at time of booking: 0.1% of sale value (paid at booking). Balance stamp duty on final sale deed: 6% of sale value (male buyer) or 5% (female buyer). Registration charge: 1% (max ₹30,000).

Occupancy Certificate (OC)
The single most important document — without OC, nothing is legal
Legal Position: Under RERA Section 11(4)(b), a promoter cannot hand over possession of a flat to a buyer without first obtaining OC. Handing over before OC is a criminal offence. Banks will not disburse home loans to buyers without OC. Buyers' advocate will insist on OC before registration of sale deed.
Steps 9.1–9.3

OC Application — What NMC Inspects

Documents to submit with OC application:

  • Architect's Completion Certificate (building constructed per sanctioned plan — signed and CoA stamped)
  • Structural Engineer's Stability Certificate (building is structurally safe for occupation)
  • Fire Department Final NOC (fire systems installed and inspected)
  • Lift Inspector's Certificate (lift installed and tested — DISH Maharashtra)
  • Rainwater Harvesting Completion Certificate
  • Solar Water Heating Installation Certificate
  • MSEDCL electrical test certificate
  • NMC water and drainage connection confirmation

NMC Final Inspection: A team (typically Town Planning Officer + Junior Engineer) visits every floor. They physically measure setbacks, check if the building matches the sanctioned plan, inspect fire exits, check parapet height, inspect parking area, verify lift room, check rainwater harvesting pit. Any deviation from the sanctioned plan is flagged.

Handling deviations: Minor deviations (e.g., a room slightly larger than plan) can be regularized by paying a compounding charge to NMC. Major structural deviations (extra floor, different layout) require demolition and reconstruction to match the sanctioned plan — this is why you must build exactly per the approved plan.

Step 11.1–11.4

Mandatory Labour Registrations for Construction

  • BOCW Registration (Building & Other Construction Workers Act, 1996): Register your project with the Maharashtra Building & Other Construction Workers Welfare Board. Pay BOCW Cess = 1% of total construction cost. This funds worker welfare — hospitals, insurance, children's education. Non-payment = government stops your construction and levies heavy penalty. Apply at: bocwwb.maharashtra.gov.in
  • ESIC (Employees' State Insurance): If you directly employ workers (not through contractor), ESIC registration required for employees earning below ₹21,000/month. Contractor's workers are contractor's obligation — but check your contract clause. ESIC contribution: Employer 3.25% + Employee 0.75% of wages. Register at: esic.in

  • Safety Officer: For construction projects above a certain size (500+ workers), a qualified Safety Officer appointment is mandatory under The Building and Other Construction Workers (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act. Maintain accident register, safety board on site.

14
Co-operative Housing Society Formation
Mandatory within 4 months of majority of flats being sold — Maharashtra law
Step 14.1–14.5 — Complete Process

How to Form the Housing Society

Governed by: Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act, 1960 and Rules 1961. Registering authority: District Deputy Registrar of Co-operative Societies, Nashik.

  1. Promoter's obligation: Under Section 10 of MOFA 1963, the builder (promoter) must take steps to form a co-operative society of the flat purchasers within 4 months of majority of flats being sold. If builder delays, buyers can form it independently.
  2. Minimum requirements: Minimum 10 members (or 60% of total flats, whichever is less) to form a society
  3. Preliminary meeting: All flat purchasers meet, pass a resolution to form a society, choose a name ("Indraprastha Heights Co-operative Housing Society"), elect provisional committee (Chairman, Secretary, Treasurer)
  4. Documents for registration:
    • Application Form A (prescribed under MCS Rules)
    • List of members with addresses and share amounts
    • Draft bye-laws in prescribed format (MCS model bye-laws 2013)
    • Minutes of preliminary meeting
    • No-objection from builder that the society is being formed
    • Proof of membership — registered agreement for sale of each member
    • Bank account opening proof (society's bank account)
    • Application fee: ₹500–2,500
  5. Registration: District Deputy Registrar processes application, verifies documents, issues Registration Certificate. Society is now a legal entity. Issue share certificate (Form J) to each member: typically 5 shares at ₹50 each = ₹250 per flat.
15
Conveyance of Land to Society
Transfer of land ownership from builder to society — often delayed by builders
Step 15.1–15.3

Conveyance Deed — What It Is and How to Execute It

After society is formed, the builder must execute a Conveyance Deed transferring the land and building to the society. This gives the society legal ownership of the property. Governed by MOFA Section 11.

  • Who executes: Builder (promoter) as seller + Society (through its Secretary authorized by General Body resolution) as purchaser
  • Stamp duty on conveyance: Varies — currently ₹100 fixed stamp duty if executed within the prescribed time limit under MOFA. Delayed conveyance attracts full stamp duty on market value.
  • Registration: Conveyance deed must be registered at Sub-Registrar office
  • After conveyance: Society's name appears in 7/12/Property Card as owner. Society can then mortgage, redevelop, or deal with the property independently.

If Builder Refuses / Delays: Society can apply for Deemed Conveyance under Section 11(3) of MCS Act. Apply to District Deputy Registrar who convenes a hearing and can order deemed conveyance. Process: 6–18 months. Increasingly common in Maharashtra — builders routinely delay conveyance to retain land control.

Advice: Execute conveyance promptly after society formation. Delays create legal complications, devalue the property in buyers' eyes, and can be used as grounds for complaints to MahaRERA.
